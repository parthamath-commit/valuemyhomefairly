Counties value every home at once. Yours can be off.

Mass-appraisal formulas can't see your dated kitchen, the busy road, or that the house next door sold for less. When the County's number is too high, you pay extra every year until someone challenges it.

Ohio lets every owner challenge it once a year, from January 1 to March 31. The strongest evidence is recent sales of similar homes nearby. Most people never file because they don't know whether they'd win. That's what the free check answers.

How it works

Three steps. No lawyers, no share of your savings.

  1. We check your value

    Your County value against 3–5 recent sales of similar homes nearby, plus a scan of your County record for errors like wrong square footage.

  2. You get a straight answer

    Likely too high, borderline, or fair. If it's fair, we tell you not to file, because an appeal can also raise a value.

  3. You file, prepared

    If it's worth it, get an evidence packet: comparable sales, the complaint form filled in, a checklist and a short hearing script. You sign and file it yourself.

Questions

Is this the County Auditor?

No. Value My Home Fairly is an independent service based in Cincinnati, not affiliated with any county, Auditor or Board of Revision.

Are you lawyers or appraisers?

No. We prepare factual information from public records so you can file your own complaint. We don't file for you, represent you or give legal advice. For complex or commercial cases we'll point you to an attorney.

Could appealing raise my value?

Yes. The Board can keep, lower or raise a value. That's why the check tells you when not to file.

How much will I save?

It depends on how far your value is above the market and on your local tax rate. As a rough guide, each $50,000 of overvaluation costs about $1,000 a year. Your free check gives you your own estimate.

When can I file?

Between January 1 and March 31, with your county Board of Revision. Join the list and we'll remind you.

What happens to my information?

We use your address to look up public property records and your email to send your result and reminders. We never sell or share it, and we'll delete it on request.
